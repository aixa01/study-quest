What is ?

turns STEM learning into an adventure. Choose from five tracks, Math, Computer Science, Physics, Biology, and Chemistry, and work through short modules that build from "what is this subject?" to the equations and ideas behind it, and finally to how it shows up in the real world. Every quiz you pass earns XP, every level brings a new pet companion, and your Learning Log keeps track of how far you've come.

Who is this for?

Anyone curious, but especially students. Research shows people learn better when they actively take part, so instead of reading passively, you answer questions, get instant feedback, explore interactive tools, and see your progress grow.

Why I built it

Growing up, and even now in college, I've struggled to make certain topics stick. I wanted a place to review what I'd learned in class, or get a head start on something new, that felt less like homework and more like a game. isn't meant to replace your classes. It's a companion to them. Here, students can learn for the sake of enjoyment, without the pressure of grades or the fear of getting something wrong.

How it was built

I built on my own during HackPrinceton Fall 2026.

  • Frontend: HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, bundled with Vite
  • Accounts & saving: Firebase Authentication (Google sign-in) and Cloud Firestore
  • Live answers: the Wolfram|Alpha API, through a Vercel serverless function
  • Hosting: Vercel

Why gamify learning?

Research shows people learn more when they actively take part, instead of just listening or reading. uses game design to turn studying into something you do.

Active learning works better

Researchers at Carnegie Mellon University's Human-Computer Interaction Institute reviewed studies on active learning. They found that interactive activities, discussion, feedback, and AI-enhanced technology led to better academic performance than traditional lectures.

They also found the most effective methods are not just "hands-on" and "minds-on," but "hearts-on": they give learners emotional and social support.

Carnegie Mellon University, "New Research Shows Learning Is More Effective When Active," October 4, 2021. Read the article

What is gamification?

eLearning Industry defines gamification as using game design features, mechanics, and concepts in education to create more engaging and fun learning environments. Common elements include points, badges, leaderboards, avatars, performance graphs, teammates, meaningful stories, and daily or weekly challenges.

Gamification adds game elements to a learning experience. That is different from game-based learning, where the whole lesson is a game.

Peterson's, "Gamification in Education: Pros, Cons, and Practical Insights," November 4, 2024. Read the article
